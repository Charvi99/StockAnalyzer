Let's get one thing straight: **There is no such thing as an "always winning" strategy.**

Anyone who tells you that is selling you snake oil. Trading is not about finding a magic pattern that never fails. It's about finding high-probability setups and, most importantly, **managing your risk** so that your wins are bigger than your losses. My "always winning" strategy is being a good risk manager, not a perfect predictor.

### 🧠 Before the Strategies: The Core Concepts

Before I show you a single chart, you need to understand two things.

#### 1. What About Splits & Dividends?
You asked about them, so let's clear this up. For a technical swing trader, they are **noise**.

* **Stock Splits:** A split is just cutting a $100 bill into two $50s. It doesn't change the value of the company. It's not a buy or sell signal.
* **Dividends:** The "dividend capture" strategy is a sucker's game. On the ex-dividend date, the stock's price is *reduced* by the dividend amount. It's a wash.

We focus on price action, volume, and momentum. We trade what the market *is doing*, not what a company's accounting department decides.

#### 2. The *Only* Strategy That Matters: Risk/Reward
Your *real* job is to be a risk manager. Every single trade you take **must** have a predefined plan.

* **Stop-Loss (SL):** This is your "I was wrong" price. You MUST have one for every trade. This is not optional.
* **Take-Profit (TP):** This is your "I was right" price.
* **The Golden Rule:** We **never** risk more than we stand to gain. We always look for a **Risk/Reward (R/R) ratio** of at least **1:2**, preferably 1:3+. This means for every $1 you risk, you aim to make $2 or $3.

If you have a 1:2 R/R, you only need to be right 34% of the time to be profitable. That's the real secret.

## 📈 My 5 Core Swing Trading Setups

These are not "always winning," but they have a positive expectancy when traded with discipline.

### 1. The Bread & Butter (Support/Resistance Flip)
This is the most fundamental pattern in trading.
* **Concept:** Old resistance, once broken, becomes new support. We're looking to buy the *first retest* of that broken level.
* **Indicators:** Price Action, Volume.
* **Example (Bullish):**
    * **Entry:** A stock breaks *above* a strong resistance level (e.g., $50). It then pulls back and *touches* $50. You buy when it shows strength *at* that $50 level.
    * **Stop-Loss:** Just below that $50 level (e.g., $49.50).
    * **Take-Profit:** At the next visible resistance level, or at a 2x or 3x multiple of your risk (e.g., $51.50 or $53).

### 2. The Trend-Rider's Dip (MA Pullback)
"The trend is your friend." This strategy helps you join an existing trend.
* **Concept:** In a strong uptrend, stocks don't go up in a straight line. They pull back. We use a key **Moving Average (MA)**, like the 50-day SMA, as a dynamic "buy zone."
* **Indicators:** 50-day Simple Moving Average (SMA), 20-day Exponential Moving Average (EMA), Overall Trend.
* **Example (Bullish):**
    * **Entry:** A stock is in a clear uptrend (price is above the rising 50 SMA). It pulls back *to* the 50 SMA and prints a bullish candlestick (like a hammer or bullish engulfing).
    * **Stop-Loss:** Just below the low of that signal candle or below the 50 SMA.
    * **Take-Profit:** At the previous swing high, or a 1:2 R/R.

### 3. The Coiled Spring (Consolidation Breakout)
Big moves often come after periods of rest. We trade the "explosion" out of that rest.
* **Concept:** After a strong move up, a stock will pause and trade sideways in a tight pattern (like a **bull flag**, **pennant**, or **ascending triangle**). This is the market "coiling" for its next move.
* **Indicators:** Chart Patterns (Flags, Triangles), Volume (should be low during consolidation and *high* on the breakout).
* **Example (Bullish Flag):**
    * **Entry:** Wait for the price to **break out** of the top of the flag pattern on high volume.
    * **Stop-Loss:** Below the *low* of the breakout candle or inside the flag pattern.
    * **Take-Profit:** A "measured move," which is the length of the initial flagpole added to the breakout point.

### 4. The Divergence Reversal (RSI)
This is how we spot a trend that is running out of steam and is ready to reverse.
* **Concept:** **Bullish Divergence** is when the price makes a *new low*, but a momentum indicator like the **Relative Strength Index (RSI)** makes a *higher low*. This shows that the selling momentum is fading, and a snap-back rally is likely.
* **Indicators:** RSI (14-period), Price Action.
* **Example (Bullish Reversal):**
    * **Entry:** The stock price hits $20. It bounces, then falls to $19. *However*, the RSI at $20 was 25, and at $19, it's 30. This is bullish divergence. You buy when the price breaks its mini-downtrend line.
    * **Stop-Loss:** Just below the new low (e.g., $18.90).
    * **Take-Profit:** At the first major resistance level.

### 5. The Channel Surfer (Range Trading)
Not all stocks trend. Many get "stuck" in a predictable range. We can profit from that.
* **Concept:** A stock is bouncing neatly between two parallel trendlines (support and resistance). This is a "channel." The strategy is simple: **Buy at the bottom (support) and sell at the top (resistance).**
* **Indicators:** Parallel Trendlines (drawing the channel), RSI (to confirm overbought at the top / oversold at the bottom).
* **Example (Range-Bound):**
    * **Entry:** A stock has reliably bounced off $100 and been rejected at $110, multiple times. When it pulls back to $100 and shows a strong buy signal, you go long.
    * **Stop-Loss:** Just below the channel support (e.g., $99.50).
    * **Take-Profit:** At the top of the channel (e.g., $109.50).

These are my go-to setups. They aren't magic, and they will all have losing trades. Your success will not be defined by *which* one you pick, but by how **disciplined** you are in your execution, your stop-losses, and your risk management.
